Help paying for Scouting

Cost should never be the reason a Scout misses camp, NYLT or a trip, or a leader misses Wood Badge. Help exists at the unit, council and national level. Here's what's available, when to apply, and how to ask without anyone else knowing.

Ask for help privately

Needing help is common, and no family should ever have to explain their situation in front of others. Here's the quiet way to ask.

For families

  1. Talk to one leader you trust.Your Cubmaster, Scoutmaster, committee chair or advancement chair. A text or a word after a meeting is enough: "Could we talk privately about help paying for camp?"
  2. Ask about unit help first.Many units set aside money for families who need it, or let Scouts build a Scout account through popcorn and camp card sales.
  3. Apply to your council.Camperships and fee help are applied for directly with the council, not through other families. Links are below.
  4. Apply early.Most help is first come, first served, and some councils ask you to apply before you pay any camp fees.

For unit leaders: set up a private request form

A simple online form lets families ask without a face-to-face conversation. Any adult can set one up in about 20 minutes.

  1. Pick one or two trusted adultswho will see the answers, such as the committee chair and the Scoutmaster or Cubmaster. Nobody else gets access.
  2. Create a short formwith a free tool such as Google Forms or Microsoft Forms. Ask only: the Scout's name, what they'd like to attend, and the best way to reach a parent. Don't ask for income or documents; the council handles eligibility.
  3. Keep answers private.Turn off any option that shows a summary of responses to people who fill it out, and never discuss requests in group chats or meetings.
  4. Share it everywhere, for everyone.Put the link in every newsletter, camp packet and sign-up email, and on a QR code at parent meetings, so asking feels normal rather than singled out.
  5. Reply within a week,then help the family apply to the council and unit funds.
  6. Clear it out each season.Delete responses once camp season ends, and write the form's owner and settings into your unit's hand-off notes.

National help

  • Military Family Fee Waiver. Announced by Scouting America on June 8, 2026. It waives national registration fees for youth in Cub Scouting, Scouts BSA, Venturing and Sea Scouting whose parent or guardian serves on active duty, in the Reserves or in the National Guard. Apply through your council.
  • Eagle Scout and college scholarships. See the NESA and Scouting America scholarship links above, plus council scholarships such as Del-Mar-Va's.
  • Wood Badge for adults. Many employers, units and chartered organizations pay all or part of the fee, since it's recognized leadership training. It never hurts to ask.
