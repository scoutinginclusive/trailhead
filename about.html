About Scouting Out Scouts

Our vision: every Scout gets the full Scouting experience, whatever their family's finances, background or ability to pay. This site turns that vision into systems that keep working long after one volunteer's ticket is complete.

Built on the Scout Oath and Law

The Scout Oath asks us to help other people at all times. Every page on this site is held to the Scout Law, the same standard we ask of our Scouts.

Trustworthy

Every date, fee and link comes from an official council or Scouting America page and is checked every Monday. When something isn't confirmed, we say so.

Helpful

Written for the family who has never heard the word "den" and the volunteer with ten minutes to spare. Plain words, with every term explained.

Friendly and Courteous

Welcoming to every family, whatever their background, budget or experience. Asking for help is private and dignified.

Kind

Nothing here is meant to make anyone feel behind. Help is offered quietly and freely.

Thrifty

Swap, borrow, earn your own way, and find the camperships and scholarships that already exist, so cost is never why a Scout stays home.

Clean

Content standards, an approved-sites-only link list and an automatic safety check keep everything appropriate for a Cub Scout and their grandparents.

Wood Badge in action

Wood Badge is Scouting America's advanced leadership course. Its five themes guided how this project was built.

ThemeWhat it meansHow this project lives it
Living the ValuesBeing the values and setting the exampleAccurate, honest, kind and thrifty, in every listing and every page.
GrowingKnowing yourself and committing to lifelong learningEach goal grew from real experience as an advancement chair and as a parent of NYLT Scouts.
ConnectingBuilding relationships with other peopleLinks ten councils, local families through the swap group, and parents directly to opportunities.
GuidingEnabling and developing othersStep-by-step guides and EDGE (Explain, Demonstrate, Guide, Enable) for training new helpers and counselors.
EmpoweringHelping others become capable and able to leadEvery system has a how-to and a hand-off, so any adult can run it and nothing depends on one person.

Each ticket goal was also written as a SMART goal (specific, measurable, attainable, relevant and timely), and each answers the three questions every Wood Badge ticket asks: does it promote diversity, does it help membership grow and Scouts stay, and does it show a clear line of sight to the youth?

How it keeps going

A ticket ends. These systems are built not to.

  • Weekly automatic update. Every Monday, new courses and events from ten councils are added, and past ones are removed.
  • Weekly link check. Every link on the site is checked. Broken, moved or retired links are reported to a person, never silently changed.
  • Safety check. No update goes live unless it passes an automatic check for accuracy, approved links and appropriate content.
  • Written standards. Clear content standards, grounded in the Scout Law, apply to people and automation alike.
  • Hand-off guide. Plain instructions so any volunteer can take over, fix a listing or refresh the yearly calendar.
  • Open and free. The whole site is free to host and open on GitHub, so other districts and councils can copy it for their own families.

Lend a hand

Spreading the load is part of Scouting. A few small jobs keep this resource strong:

  • Read the Monday update email and follow up on any flagged links.
  • Refresh the Help paying calendar each fall.
  • Run a uniform bin in your unit.
  • Become a merit badge counselor, or mentor someone who will.
  • Review Spanish translations as they're added.
