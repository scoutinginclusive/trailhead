Safe Scouting

Every Scout deserves to be safe, and every adult shares that job. These are reminders of the most important rules, with links to Scouting America's official documents, which are always the final word.

The official documents are the rulebook

This page is a plain-language summary to help families and leaders find the rules. It is not the rules themselves. Scouting America's official documents, starting with the Guide to Safe Scouting, are the official source. Read them, and use them for every final decision, policy or change. If anything here ever differs from an official document, the official document is correct.

If a child may be in danger

  1. In an emergency, call 911.Get the child to safety and get medical help if needed.
  2. Report suspected abuse to law enforcement or child protective services.This comes first, and every adult has the responsibility to report.
  3. Call the Scouts First Helpline: 844-726-8871(844-SCOUTS1), available 24 hours, for suspected abuse or serious youth protection concerns in Scouting America. All youth protection violations must also be reported to your local council.

Training every adult should take

  • Safeguarding Youth training (formerly called Youth Protection Training) is required for every registered adult and must be renewed every year. A volunteer whose training has lapsed can't re-register. It's free and online at my.scouting.org.
  • Parents and guardians can take it too, without registering, and are strongly encouraged to. A Spanish-language version is available.
  • Hazardous Weather training is required every two years for leaders who work directly with youth.
  • Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat are required, within the previous two years, for adults supervising swimming or boating.
  • Position-specific training for your role, plus courses like BALOO or IOLS before leading campouts. See upcoming courses near you.

Scouting's barriers to abuse

These policies protect youth and adults alike. They apply to every Scouting activity: meetings, campouts, service projects and online.

Two-deep leadership

At least two registered adult leaders, 21 or older, are required at every Scouting activity.

No one-on-one contact

No adult may be alone with a youth who isn't their own child, in person, online, by phone or by text. Copy another adult or a parent on messages.

Buddy system

Scouts pair up and stay together during activities, especially outdoors and around water.

Screened and trained adults

Every registered adult is approved by the chartered organization, has a criminal background check, completes Safeguarding Youth training and follows the Scouter Code of Conduct.

Tenting, lodging and showers

  • Youth sharing a tent must be no more than two years apart in age.
  • Separate accommodations are required for male and female adults and for male and female youth, for tenting, lodging and restrooms.
  • Adults don't share a tent with youth. Adults 18 to 20 count as adults for this rule. The exception: in Cub Scouting, a parent or guardian may tent with their own family.
  • No adult shares a tent with someone of the opposite sex other than their spouse.
  • Showers and restrooms: separate facilities whenever possible; otherwise, separate shower times are scheduled and posted. Adults enter youth changing or shower areas only as needed for health and safety.
  • Cabins and shared lodging have their own detailed rules, including when adults and youth may share a room. Check the Guide to Safe Scouting before you book.

Your council or a camp may add stricter rules. When in doubt, ask your council and follow the stricter rule.

Health forms

  • Annual Health and Medical Record (AHMR): every participant, youth and adult, needs Parts A and B for all events, reviewed at least once a year.
  • Part C, completed by a licensed healthcare provider, is required for events longer than 72 hours and for high-adventure bases. Some councils require it more often.
