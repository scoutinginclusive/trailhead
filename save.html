Scout It Out · Gear & Uniform Swap

A private group where Scouting families swap uniforms, books and camping gear for free or a fair price. Families from every Scouting organization are welcome.

It's private to protect members. Tap the button, choose Join group, and answer three short questions; an admin approves each request.

The group's rules
  • Be kind, courteous and friendly. No politics or negativity.
  • Keep our youth safe: adults only, no Scout names or faces, adults arrange every swap.
  • Free first, fair always. No reselling or businesses.
  • Be trustworthy: describe items honestly.
  • Meet in public places, and share addresses by private message only.
  • Follow through, and mark items claimed.
  • Scouting items only. Earned awards stay earned, and knives, axes and fuel pass adult to adult. No firearms or ammunition.
  • Pay it forward when your Scout outgrows something.
  • Admins keep it safe.

Run a uniform bin in your unit

A uniform bin is a tote of outgrown uniform pieces that travels to every meeting. Families take what they need and leave what they've outgrown. No sign-out and no questions asked. Any adult can run one using these steps.

What you need

  • One lidded plastic tote that fits in a car
  • Gallon zip bags and a marker for sizes
  • A sign for the lid: "Take what you need. Leave what you've outgrown."
  • A one-page note taped inside the lid with these steps and your name

Steps

  1. Get the committee's OKand agree who keeps the bin between meetings.
  2. Collect.Announce it at meetings, at bridging, and at the end of the year when Scouts outgrow the most.
  3. Sort and labelby program (Cub Scouts, Scouts BSA) and size, one zip bag per size.
  4. Bring it everywhere:meetings, bridging ceremonies and recruiting nights. Seeing it often makes using it feel normal.
  5. Keep it fresh.Once a month, wash anything that needs it and recycle what's worn out.
  6. Pass it on.When you step back, hand the bin and its lid note to the next volunteer.

Earned awards stay with the Scout who earned them. The bin is for uniform pieces, neckerchiefs, belts and general gear.

Buy smart

Before you buy anything

  • Ask your unit leader which items your Scout actually needs this month. The rest can wait.
  • Check the swap group and your unit's uniform bin first.
  • Borrow before you buy: many units have loaner tents, packs and stoves for a first campout.
  • Buy shirts and pants with room to grow.

Camping gear on a budget

  • Buy gear in stages, as real trips call for it, rather than all at once.
  • Look at thrift stores, garage sales and the used-gear sections some outdoor stores run.
  • Ask experienced families what they'd skip. They'll often lend or give what they no longer use.

Earn your way

Scouting teaches young people to pay their own way, and units have approved ways to help them do it.

Council product sales

Popcorn and camp cards are run by your council and are already approved. Many units put part of each Scout's sales into a Scout account for camp and trips. Ask how your unit handles it.

Your own unit fundraiser

  • Any fundraiser other than council product sales needs approval. Submit the Unit Money-Earning Application (form 34427) to your council, usually at least two weeks ahead.
  • Units earn money by selling products or providing a service, such as a car wash.
  • Only Scouting America and councils may ask for donations in the name of Scouting, so units and Scouts don't ask businesses or individuals for cash gifts.
  • Keep unit money in a unit bank account, with two signatures on every check.
