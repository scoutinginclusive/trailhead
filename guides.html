Keeping advancement up to date in Scoutbook Plus

When records fall behind, Scouts can lose credit for work they've already done, and boards of review get harder to schedule. A simple routine shared by a few adults keeps every Scout's record current, so Scouts and parents can see real progress.

Who does what

  • Your unit's Key 3 decides who can enter and approve advancement, using Scoutbook Plus permissions.
  • Advancement chair and helpers enter completed requirements, ranks and merit badges.
  • Parents can connect to their Scout's record to follow progress, using Scouting America's parent tutorial.

The routine

  • Within a week of each meeting or campout: enter what Scouts completed.
  • Before each court of honor or pack meeting: approve items, then create the purchase order and advancement report.
  • After badges are handed out: mark them awarded.
  • Every few months: run each Scout's report and compare it with their handbook.
  • Every August: review this toolkit and train any new helpers.

Recording and approving advancement

  1. Sign inat advancements.scouting.org, open the Dashboard, and choose your unit.
  2. Open the Scout.Click their name on the roster, then View More on the rank, merit badge or award.
  3. Mark requirements.Approve individual requirements, or complete the whole rank or badge. Quick Entry (upper right) handles several at once.
  4. Approve.On the To Approve tab, check the items and click Approve Items.
  5. Order.On the To Purchase tab, check the items, click Add items to order, then View Order to produce the Advancement Report or purchase order for the Scout Shop.
  6. Award.After the Scout receives the badge, use the To Award tab and click Award Items.

Two mistakes to avoid

Don't mark items as awarded while they're still on an open purchase order; that removes them from the order. To fix a wrong date, delete it and enter the correct one.

Training the next helper

Wood Badge teaches a four-step way to pass on any skill, called EDGE. Use it to make sure advancement never depends on just one person.

Explain

Why records matter and how the routine works. Share this page.

Demonstrate

Enter a real meeting's advancement together, while they watch.

Guide

They enter the next meeting while you sit beside them and answer questions.

Enable

They take a meeting on their own, and know who to ask. Add them to your unit's hand-off notes.

Becoming a merit badge counselor

Merit badges let Scouts try things they might never get to do otherwise, from welding to weather. Every badge needs a counselor, and a shortage of counselors is often what slows Scouts down on the way to Eagle. If you have a hobby, a career or a skill you love, you can share it.

How to apply

  1. Choose your badges.Pick subjects you know well from work, hobbies or experience. Start with one or two.
  2. Register as an adultif you aren't already, using the adult application. A registration fee may apply; your council can tell you.
  3. Fill out the Merit Badge Counselor Information form(No. 34405), listing each badge and what qualifies you for it.
  4. Complete Youth Protection training(Safeguarding Youth) at my.scouting.org.
  5. Take Merit Badge Counselor Trainingin the training section of my.scouting.org.
  6. Submit to your council.Bring or mail the forms rather than emailing them, since the application contains personal information. A district or council advancement leader approves you.
  7. Start counseling.Once approved, you appear in Scoutbook for the badges you were approved for, usually within a few days.

Some badges need extra qualifications

Aquatics, shooting sports, climbing, snow sports and scuba badges require specific certifications. See section 7.0.1.1 of the Guide to Advancement before signing up for these.

Counseling well

  • Scouts never meet a counselor alone. Another adult or a buddy is always present, in person or online.
  • Requirements are completed exactly as written: no more and no less. If a requirement says "demonstrate," the Scout shows you.
  • Share your experience. The best counselors make the subject come alive.

A mentor's prep conversation

Recruiting a new counselor goes best as a friendly conversation, not a sign-up sheet. Use these prompts with any adult you'd like to bring in.

Ask

  • What do you do for work, or love doing on weekends?
  • Which of those connects to a merit badge? Browse the list together.
  • How much time could you give: one Scout a season, or a group workshop?

Share

  • A story of a Scout who earned a badge with real help from a counselor.
  • What a typical first meeting looks like.
  • That support is available: other counselors and the district advancement team.

Do together

  • Open form 34405 and fill it out side by side.
  • Set a date to finish both trainings.
  • Agree on a follow-up check-in two weeks later.

Eagle-required merit badges

Eagle Scout takes 21 merit badges: these 13 required badges plus 8 electives of the Scout's choice. Where a line offers a choice, only one badge from that line counts as required, and an extra one can count as an elective.

  • Camping
  • Citizenship in the Community
  • Citizenship in the Nation
  • Citizenship in the World
  • Communication
  • Cooking
  • Family Life
  • First Aid
  • Personal Fitness
  • Personal Management
  • Emergency Preparedness or Lifesaving
  • Environmental Science or Sustainability
  • Swimming or Hiking, or Cycling

What changed in 2026

Citizenship in Society was discontinued on February 27, 2026, and is no longer Eagle-required. Scouts who had already started it can finish it by December 31, 2026 and still use it toward Eagle. Check the official notice for details.

For Scouts and parents

How merit badges work

  1. Choose a badgeand read its current requirements.
  2. Talk with your unit leader,who signs your merit badge application (the "blue card") and helps you find a counselor.
  3. Contact the counselorand set up a first meeting. Always bring a buddy or have another adult present.
  4. Do the requirementsexactly as written. Your counselor signs off on each one.
  5. Turn in your signed applicationto your unit leader so the badge is recorded and you receive it.

There's no deadline to finish a badge, as long as all the work is done before a Scout's 18th birthday. Merit badge days, workshops and colleges are a great way to start; see what's coming up.
